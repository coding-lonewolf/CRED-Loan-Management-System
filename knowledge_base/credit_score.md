# Credit-Score Impact Factors
Document Type: Credit Education / Knowledge-Base Article
Market: India
Applicable Credit Bureaus: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark
Effective Date: 31 October 2026
Version: 1.0
Description
A credit score is a numerical summary of information in an individual's credit history and is used by lenders as one factor when assessing a credit application. In India, credit information companies such as TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark maintain credit records and generate scores using their own scoring models. Repayment behaviour is an important factor, and missed or delayed payments on credit cards, personal loans, home loans, or other reported credit facilities can adversely affect a credit profile. Keeping payments on or before their due dates helps establish a consistent repayment record. Credit utilisation, which is the proportion of available revolving credit being used, can also influence a score; regularly using a large share of the available credit limit may indicate higher credit risk. The age of credit accounts and the overall mix of credit, such as secured loans and unsecured credit, may also be considered by a bureau's scoring model. Several credit applications within a short period can lead to multiple hard enquiries, which may affect the score and may also signal increased demand for credit to lenders. Closing an old account, settling a loan, or reducing an outstanding balance does not necessarily produce an immediate or predictable score change. The effect of any action depends on the individual's complete credit file, the information reported by lenders, and the scoring methodology used by the relevant bureau. Customers should check their credit reports periodically for incorrect account details, unfamiliar enquiries, duplicate entries, or payment statuses that have not been updated. If an error is found, the customer should raise a dispute with the relevant credit bureau and, where appropriate, the lender that supplied the information, using their official channels. No fixed point increase or decrease, approval outcome, or exact time for score recovery should be guaranteed.
Key Factors
Factor	Possible influence on a credit profile	Practical guidance
Repayment history	Missed, delayed, or defaulted payments may negatively affect the profile.	Pay at least the required amount by the due date; paying the total statement balance helps avoid revolving credit-card debt.
Credit utilisation	High usage of available credit limits may indicate greater dependence on revolving credit.	Keep balances manageable and avoid routinely using most of the available limit.
Credit history length	A longer record of responsibly managed accounts may provide more information about repayment behaviour.	Avoid closing older accounts solely to try to change the score; consider fees and account needs before making a decision.
Credit mix	A mix of secured and unsecured credit may be considered by some scoring models.	Borrow only when needed; do not take a loan merely to create a particular credit mix.
Recent credit enquiries	Multiple hard enquiries in a short period may affect the score and can be viewed by lenders as increased credit-seeking activity.	Compare offers carefully and limit unnecessary applications.
Report accuracy	Incorrect balances, account ownership, overdue status, or enquiry records can distort the credit profile.	Review reports and dispute inaccurate information through official bureau and lender channels.


Customer Guidance
- Check the credit report periodically and review all active and closed accounts.
- Pay credit-card bills and loan instalments by their respective due dates.
- Monitor credit-card balances relative to the sanctioned limits.
- Avoid submitting several credit applications within a short period unless necessary.
- Contact the lender if a payment is not correctly reflected in the credit report.
- Raise disputes through the relevant credit bureau's official process and retain the reference number and supporting documents.
Important Disclaimer
This document provides general credit-education information for a banking knowledge base. It is not a credit-scoring formula, a lender's underwriting policy, or a guarantee of loan or card approval. Credit bureaus may use different scoring models, and lenders may apply additional eligibility criteria.
